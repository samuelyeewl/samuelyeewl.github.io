While most hot Jupiters are found to be "lonely", without any nearby planets, (e.g.[Huang et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-637x/825/2/98), [Sha et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ae8bb0)), an interesting subset of them have close-by planetary companions.
One way to detect such companions is to see them transit their host stars, but non-transiting planets may be detected by their influence on the transiting hot Jupiter.
Because of gravitational interactions between the hot Jupiter and its companion, the hot Jupiter's transits may not arrive exactly like clockwork, but instead deviate in their timing (transit timing variations; TTVs) or shape (transit duration variations; TDVs).

I have been conducting a uniform search for TTVs and TDVs in the homogeneous hot Jupiter sample from the Grand Unified Survey.
Initial results include the discovery of some of the largest TTVs and TDVs ever seen for hot Jupiters.
One case is the TOI-3686 system ([Yee et al. 2026b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28533)), which contains a hot Jupiter showing peak-to-peak TTVs of 40 minutes, and an inner sub-Neptune with TTVs of a whopping 10 hours, 16% of its orbital period.
This is the first hot Jupiter system in a true mean-motion resonance with a nearby planet.

Even more spectacular is the TOI-2535 system (Yee et al. 2026a), where not only does the hot Jupiter show peak-to-peak TTVs of 12 hours, but its transits gradually got shorter and shallower, until they vanished completely. This is the first planet that we have directly observed its transits to disappear, and is the result of nodal precession induced by a nearby, mutually inclined planet. Apart from TESS data, we were able to characterize this system in detail thanks to many collaborators across five continents who observed the transits from the ground.

{% include figure.html image="toi2535_press_hd_with_data.mp4" caption="The transits of TOI-2535 b gradually decreased in duration and depth over seven years of observations, the result of a changing sky-plane inclination. These orbital changes are due to the presence of a mutually inclined giant planet perturber, causing both planets' orbits to precess around the system invariable plane." width="full" %}

Other examples have been discovered by my search, including a planet whose transits have "appeared" for TESS, a nice complement to the disappearing transits of TOI-2535. I am also working on measuring the frequency of TTVs in hot Jupiter systems.
