One underexplored axis in exoplanet demographics is how planet formation and occurrence depend on galactic context. For example, our Milky Way contains three major populations of stars that differ in their kinematics and chemistry: the thin disk, thick disk, and halo. The different environments of stellar and planet formation, as well as the billions of years of evolution that the older thick disk and halo populations have experienced, likely affect the frequency and types of planets in each population.

I have been laying the groundwork to perform detailed demographics studies of giant planets in these different galactic contexts. Amongst the early results include the [discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06069) of the first transiting brown dwarf around a thick disk star, led by Harvard PhD student [Jéa Adams
Redai](https://jearedai.com/). With [Victoria DiTomasso](https://sites.google.com/view/victoria-ditomasso), now an NPP Fellow at NASA Goddard, we conducted a [homogeneous spectroscopic analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22254) of hot Jupiter host stars to identify new confirmed and candidate planets in the galactic thick disk, finding tentative differences in the properties of the two populations. We have since confirmed more such planets to be published soon (DiTomasso, Yee et al. in prep.).

Stay tuned for further updates in this area! I am actively working on understanding the relative occurrence rates of giant planets around thick disk and thin disk stars in the metal-poor regime.
